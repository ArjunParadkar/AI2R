Every reference below was checked against a real page (arXiv, proceedings or publisher) in this pass; \texttt{refs.bib} keeps the verification URL for each entry. We state in one sentence how each differs from this work.

\paragraph{Discovering or measuring symmetries and invariances.}
LieSD~\cite{liesd} recovers the Lie algebra of a trained network's continuous symmetries from inputs, outputs and gradients; our discovery targets a discrete group (a 113-cycle of token relabellings) with a mean-accepted, null-calibrated rule, because exact matching finds nothing on real networks. LieGAN~\cite{liegan} discovers a dataset's symmetries with an adversarial generator of Lie-algebra transformations; we analyse a trained network's function, not its data distribution. LieGG~\cite{lieg} extracts a trained network's learned symmetries as Lie generators and is our continuous-symmetry baseline; it does not address discrete groups, memorizer controls, or subtraction of the untrained network. Augerino~\cite{augerino} learns invariances jointly with the weights, whereas we read them off a finished model. Lenc and Vedaldi~\cite{lenc} and Goodfellow et al.~\cite{goodfellow2009} measure equivariance and invariance of learned features to image transformations; our extractor does the same on a ResNet-18 but subtracts an untrained copy to remove what the architecture gives for free. Bouchacourt et al.~\cite{bouchacourt2021} ask where invariance comes from (data, augmentation, architecture); Kvinge et al.~\cite{kvinge2022} define invariance and equivariance metrics decoupled from accuracy; Deng et al.~\cite{deng2022} relate a label-free effective invariance to generalization. None of these tests for a \emph{recoverable group} versus memorizers, or ties group acceptance to the grokking transition.

\paragraph{Transformation consistency for error detection.}
Bahat and Shakhnarovich~\cite{bahat2020} use predictions under semantics-preserving test-time augmentations to estimate confidence, and Golan and El-Yaniv~\cite{golan2018} score anomalies with transformation-classifier softmax statistics; both use fixed, hand-chosen transformations, while our $\kappa$ uses the group the network itself was found to respect, with random transformations as the null (at chance here). Hendrycks and Gimpel~\cite{hendrycks2017} and ODIN~\cite{liang2018} are the softmax baselines we compare against.

\paragraph{Grokking and algebraic tasks.}
Power et al.~\cite{power2022} introduced grokking on small algorithmic datasets. Nanda et al.~\cite{nanda2023} and Zhong et al.~\cite{zhong2023} reverse-engineer the Fourier (clock/pizza) algorithms of modular-addition networks; Chughtai et al.~\cite{chughtai2023} and Stander et al.~\cite{stander2024} do so for finite-group multiplication; Morwani et al.~\cite{morwani2024} show margin maximization yields Fourier and representation-theoretic features; Hwang and Park~\cite{intrinsic2026} (arXiv preprint) argue that task symmetry drives grokking and give a symmetry-based diagnostic. These work from mechanistic access to internals (level C) or theory; we recover the group from the input--output map with Jacobian access (level B), score it against memorizer and untrained controls, and report the nulls and failures. Our frequency recovery agrees with theirs on this task; we do not claim it is new.

\paragraph{Pullback geometry and symmetry in training dynamics.}
Hauser and Ray~\cite{hauser2017} and Benfenati and Marta~\cite{benfenati2023,benfenati2023b} study Riemannian metrics pulled back through networks, including their degeneracy and the equivalence classes they induce; we use the input pullback $g=J^\top J$ as an empirical instrument and test it. Kunin et al.~\cite{kunin2021} derive conservation laws from architectural symmetries in training dynamics, which concerns parameter-space symmetries rather than the input-space symmetries studied here.

\paragraph{Robustness, counterfactual consistency, spurious features, and evading monitors.}
Hein and Andriushchenko~\cite{hein2017} give instance-specific robustness bounds, which relates to our finding that nothing predicts the adversarial radius better than the margin. Counterfactual logit pairing~\cite{garg2019} penalizes logit differences between an example and its counterfactual, which resembles our interrupt penalty; ours is switched on once, only after a behavioural monitor fires, and requires the nuisance to be named. SPARE~\cite{spare} mitigates spurious features without group labels and is our reweighting baseline. Bailey et al.~\cite{bailey} show that models can evade latent-space defenses under obfuscation attacks, the general phenomenon of which our evasion demonstration is a small instance.
