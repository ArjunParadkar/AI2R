# E1 — Gauge orbits vs circuit non-identifiability (pre-registration)

Status: SPEC written 2026-08-30 **before** any training or enumeration run.
Nothing in `results/` exists yet. Changes to this file after the first run
are logged in the "Amendments" section at the bottom, never edited in place.

## Question

When a small network admits many valid mechanistic explanations (Méloux et
al. 2025), is that multiplicity (a) *gauge* — one mechanism seen through the
network's parameter symmetries (permutations of hidden units, positive
rescalings through ReLU; Grigsby et al. functional dimension; the 2025
parameter-symmetry survey) — or (b) *genuine* — several distinct mechanisms?
The bridge between the symmetry literature and the explanation-multiplicity
literature is, to our knowledge, unclaimed; that bridge is the only thing we
claim. The enumeration protocol is Méloux's; the symmetries are textbook.

## Networks

* MLP, ReLU hidden layers, single linear logit output, `f(x) = [logit > 0]`.
* Inputs in {0,1}^n, n ∈ {3, 4}. Hidden depth d ∈ {2, 3}, width w ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10}
  (same width every hidden layer).
* Functions. n=3: **XOR3** (= parity-3; the brief lists "parity" and "XOR3"
  separately — for n=3 they are the same function, so the third 3-input
  function is **(x₁∧x₂)∨x₃**, chosen because it has a 2-gate decomposition
  and is not symmetric in its inputs), **MAJ3**. n=4: **parity-4**,
  **threshold-2** (Σx ≥ 2), and one fixed-seed random 4-input function
  (`RAND4`, truth table drawn with `numpy.random.default_rng(4)`, recorded
  in results).
* Training: full-batch Adam, lr 1e-2, BCE-with-logits on all 2^n inputs, up to
  20 000 steps, stop at the first step with zero classification error **and**
  a margin |logit| ≥ 0.5 on every input (so binarisation is not sitting on a
  numerical edge). Seeds: 24 per (function, d, w) cell; seeds that fail to
  converge are recorded and excluded (count reported).
* Exhaustive enumeration is over 2^(d·w) neuron subsets. Budget: cells with
  d·w ≤ 20 are exhaustive (d=2 all widths; d=3 at w∈{4,6}). d=3 at w∈{8,10}
  is **out of scope** for this run (2^24–2^30 subsets); stated here, not
  silently dropped.

## Explanations (Méloux-style enumeration)

**Circuit.** A subset S of hidden neurons (over all hidden layers; the
output unit is always included). The complement is ablated in *all* layers at
once, two ways:

* zero-ablation: activation set to 0;
* mean-ablation: activation set to its mean over the 2^n inputs in the
  *full* network.

S is a **valid circuit** under an ablation scheme if the ablated network
reproduces f on all 2^n inputs (sign of the logit; no margin required).
Validity is recorded separately for each scheme; the two validity sets and
their symmetric difference are reported (their disagreement is data).

**Minimality (design decision).** Validity is not monotone under mean
ablation, and under zero ablation every superset of a valid circuit that adds
a dead-on-all-inputs neuron is trivially valid. Counting all valid subsets
therefore mostly counts "circuit plus junk". The **primary analysis uses
minimal valid circuits** (no proper subset is valid under the same scheme);
the all-valid count is reported alongside as a secondary column. Alternative
(not primary): count all valid subsets — we report it, so a reader who
prefers Méloux's raw count can use it.

**Interpretation.** Run the ablated network; binarise every circuit neuron
by `b = [a > 0]` (ReLU-active; scaling-invariant by construction; the
alternative — median split — is not used). For a neuron in hidden layer ℓ
its candidate parents are the circuit neurons of layer ℓ−1 (the inputs for
ℓ=1); for the output unit, the circuit neurons of the last hidden layer. A
**gate assignment** for a neuron is a parent set P (|P| ≤ 3) such that the
map (b_p)_{p∈P} ↦ b is a well-defined function on the 2^n inputs (same parent
pattern never yields two outputs) and is **minimal** (no proper subset of P
is also consistent). Gates are identified by their *partial* truth table on
the parent patterns actually observed — we do not multiply by the free
choices on unobserved patterns (that would inflate counts with fictions). A
neuron that is constant over all inputs has the single assignment P = ∅
("constant gate"). An **interpretation** of a circuit is a choice of one
gate assignment per neuron; the interpretation count of S is
∏_neurons (#minimal consistent parent sets). A valid circuit with a neuron
that has no consistent parent set of size ≤ 3 has zero interpretations and
is recorded as "valid but uninterpretable" — also data.

**Explanation** = (valid minimal circuit S, interpretation I).
`N_expl(net, scheme) = Σ_S N_interp(S)`.

## Symmetry group and gauge equivalence

G is generated by (a) permutations of hidden units within each hidden layer,
(b) per-hidden-unit positive scalings (w_in, b) → α(w_in, b), w_out → w_out/α.
(c) Hidden symmetries are *detected* (below), not enumerated.

G acts on explanations: g·(S, I) = (g S, g I) — relabel the neurons. On a
single generic trained network the stabiliser of θ in G is trivial, so
literally distinct subsets are literally distinct orbits and the ratio would
be 1 by construction. That is not the question. The question is whether two
explanations are *the same mechanism up to gauge*, i.e. whether the
**parameter-implementation of the circuit** — the weights restricted to S:
input→S₁, S₁→S₂, …, S_d→output, with biases — of one is the image under some
g ∈ G of the other's, to tolerance. This makes redundancy (two rescaled /
relabelled copies of the same sub-circuit in one network — the Hydra
effect) gauge-equivalent, and lets us compare explanations *across seeds*.

**Canonical form (design decision).** For a circuit S:
1. scaling gauge: for each hidden neuron in S, divide (w_in, b) by
   ‖(w_in, b)‖₂ and multiply its outgoing weights by the same norm,
   layer by layer from the input; then divide the output row by its own
   norm (the output scale does not change f — recorded, not part of the key);
2. permutation gauge: within each layer, sort neurons by their normalised
   incoming vector (lexicographic), layer by layer from the input so that
   downstream columns are permuted consistently;
3. quantise every entry to a grid of step ε and hash.
Two explanations are **gauge-equivalent at ε** if their keys coincide and the
interpretations coincide under the induced neuron matching. Quantisation is
a grid, not a true ε-ball (boundary artefacts exist); ε is swept over
{0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2} and **ε = 0.05 is the pre-registered primary**.
Alternative not used: Procrustes-style optimal matching per pair
(O(N²) and needs a threshold anyway).

A coarser, exactly gauge-invariant key is also reported: the **functional
key** = per layer, the sorted multiset of binarised truth tables of the
circuit neurons, plus the output's. Two explanations with the same functional
key compute the same Boolean intermediates even if their weights differ.
This is a *secondary* notion of "same mechanism" — reported, not primary.

**Orbits** = equivalence classes of explanations under the key. Computed
(i) **within-network** (primary for H-E1a) and (ii) **pooled across the 24
seeds** of a cell (how many distinct mechanisms does training find).

**Hidden symmetries.** Functional dimension (Grigsby, Lindsey, Meyerhoff,
Wu 2022): rank of the Jacobian of θ ↦ (f_θ(x_j))_{j=1..m} at the trained θ
on m = 4·#params random inputs in [−1, 2]^n (off the Boolean cube, so the
count is about the realisation map, not the 2^n truth table). Expected rank
= #params − #hidden units (one scaling direction each; permutations are
discrete). Rank tolerance: singular values < 1e-6·σ_max are zero. Deficit
beyond that is reported per network, split into "dead units" (unit
inactive on all m inputs — a known, boring source of deficit) and
"unexplained". Unexplained deficit > 0 in more than 10 % of networks of a
cell ⇒ flag the cell; E2 then also samples those directions numerically
(null-space vectors of the Jacobian). We do not characterise them.

## Pre-registered hypotheses and pass/fail

* **H-E1a** (gauge explains ≥ 50 % of multiplicity): at ε = 0.05,
  within-network, median over converged seeds of
  `N_orbits / N_expl ≤ 0.5`, for the minimal-circuit, mean-ablation count.
  Reported for every (function, d, w) cell; the hypothesis "passes" for a
  cell if the median ratio ≤ 0.5 and the bootstrap 95 % CI of the median
  (seeds resampled) lies below 0.5. Zero-ablation and the all-valid count
  are reported as robustness columns.
* **H-E1b** (orbits grow slower than explanations with width): for each
  (function, d), fit log(count) = a + s·w across seeds for N_expl and
  N_orbits; pass if s_orbits < s_expl with a seed-bootstrap 95 % CI of the
  difference excluding 0.
* Descriptive, no pass/fail: interpretation-multiplicity share
  (Σ_S N_interp(S) / #minimal circuits), zero-vs-mean disagreement rate,
  uninterpretable-circuit rate, pooled cross-seed orbit counts, functional-key
  orbit counts, hidden-symmetry flags.

Honest prior: interpretation multiplicity (several gate readings of one
circuit) is *not* moved by G at all, and distinct circuits in one generic
network rarely coincide to ε = 0.05 unless the network is redundant. So
H-E1a may well fail — that would mean the multiplicity is genuine, which is
the result either way. We report whatever happens.

## Kill attempts and controls (run before believing anything)

1. **Invariance unit test.** For random networks: apply a random g ∈ G;
   N_expl, the set of canonical keys, and N_orbits must be identical before
   and after (exact for permutations; for scalings, identical at every ε).
2. **Hand-constructed nets with known orbits.** XOR3 built by hand from
   two hidden layers; a second copy with a duplicated (relabelled, rescaled
   ×3) sub-circuit; a third with a *different* second implementation
   (MAJ-style gates). Expected orbit counts are written in the test before
   the code runs (2 copies → 1 orbit; 2 different → 2 orbits).
3. **Lookup-table baseline.** A hand-built width-2^n one-hot network
   implementing each f (one hidden unit per input pattern, output = Σ over
   true patterns). Known structure: one minimal circuit per scheme, gate
   interpretation = the truth table itself. The enumeration must find
   exactly that; the ratio there is trivially 1 and is reported as the
   "no-gauge-possible" reference.
4. **Quantisation-boundary check.** Jitter all weights by 1e-4 relative;
   orbit counts must not change at ε ≥ 0.02 in more than 5 % of networks.
5. **Untrained control.** Random-init networks of the same shapes: they do
   not compute f, so N_expl should be 0 — a sanity check that the validity
   test is not vacuous.
6. Seeds: everything is per-seed with bootstrap CIs over seeds.

2. (2026-08-30, after a 2-cell smoke run — XOR3 d2 w4 and MAJ3 d2 w6, 3
   seeds — and before the pre-registered run) **Interpretation definition
   replaced with Méloux et al.'s actual one.** The SPEC's fixed
   `b = [a > 0]` binarisation made every minimal circuit of every trained
   network "valid but uninterpretable" (N_expl = 0): trained ReLU nets carry
   the computation in activation *magnitudes* (e.g. a unit active on all 8
   inputs whose size encodes the answer), so downstream bits and the output
   are not Boolean functions of ReLU-active bits. That is itself recorded as
   a finding (the ReLU-active reading is not available on these nets), but
   it makes H-E1a vacuous. Méloux et al. (2025, Sec. 3, "Interpretation
   search") instead choose the 0/1 threshold *per neuron as part of the
   interpretation*: given the parents' bits, "list all possible ways to
   separate these inputs", 1 = the larger interval, recursively layer by
   layer; the output bit is fixed by the behaviour. Re-implemented exactly
   so (`e1lib/enumerate.py::separations`, `interpretations`): a separation
   is any threshold between consecutive distinct activation values such
   that the bits are a function of the circuit-parents' bits; the arity cap
   and minimal-parent-set machinery are dropped (parents = all circuit
   units of the previous layer, as in their Definition 1 with node-level
   circuits). Interpretation identity for orbit keys = per-layer multisets
   of partial truth tables with parent columns in canonical (sorted) order —
   gauge-invariant by construction. Reference numbers from the paper for
   calibration: 2-input XOR, 2×3 MLP → 85 perfect circuits, 535.8
   interpretations per circuit. Smoke: one XOR3 circuit → 219
   interpretations. Hypotheses, ε grid, controls unchanged. Consequence
   made explicit: within a single generic network, interpretation
   multiplicity is untouched by G, so the within-network ratio is ≈ 1
   unless circuits are duplicated; the pooled-across-seeds orbit count is
   where gauge can act. Both were already pre-registered.
